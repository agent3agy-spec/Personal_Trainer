## 1 · Olympic weightlifting

### 1.1 What all schools share

- Competition lifts: snatch, clean and jerk. Core support: front squat, back squat, pulls, jerk variants, overhead squat. [believe]
- Intensity is expressed as %1RM of the competition lift or squat, with most volume between 70 and 85%. Soviet summaries put roughly half to two thirds of lifts there ("around 68% of lifts at 70-85%" in one summary, "half at 70-80%" in another). [snippet] Source: [Westside Barbell, Medvedyev](https://www.westside-barbell.com/a/blog/a-system-of-multi-year-training-in-weightlifting-by-a-s-medvedyev), [Breaking Muscle](https://breakingmuscle.com/fitness/4-secrets-of-soviet-weightlifting-as-revealed-by-pavel)
- Reps per set on the classic lifts stay low (1 to 3, rarely 5 on pulls and squats) because technique degrades under fatigue. [believe]

### 1.2 Prilepin's chart (Soviet, A.S. Prilepin)

| %1RM | reps per set | optimal total | total range |
|:-|:-|:-|:-|
| 55-65 | 3-6 | 24 | 18-30 |
| 70-80 | 3-6 | 18 | 12-24 |
| 80-90 | 2-4 | 15 | 10-20 |
| 90+ | 1-2 | 4 (some copies 7) | 4-10 (some copies 10) |

[snippet] Sources: [EliteFTS Hristov article](https://www.elitefts.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Hristove-article.pdf), [Lift Vault](https://liftvault.com/resources/prilepins-table-inol/), [Powerlifting Technique](https://powerliftingtechnique.com/prilepins-chart/). The 90%+ row disagrees across secondary copies (optimal 4 vs 7). Boundaries also vary (70-79/80-89/>89). It is a per session, per exercise guide for the classic lifts, derived from Soviet lifter logs, not a controlled study. Evidence: **COACH / OBS**.

### 1.3 Soviet / Russian system (Medvedyev, Vorobyev, Roman)

- **Prescribes:** multi year planning by classification level (Class II up to Master of Sport International), a wide exercise menu (many pull, squat and press variants), wave loading of volume across weeks, and fewer near maximal lifts as the athlete gets better. A translated 1986 Medvedyev table gives yearly lifts at 90-95% falling from 11 (Class II) to 2 (high sport mastery), and at 80-85% from 29 to 19. [snippet] [Westside Barbell, Medvedyev](https://www.westside-barbell.com/a/blog/a-system-of-multi-year-training-in-weightlifting-by-a-s-medvedyev)
- In a 1960s eight week cycle with 4 to 5 workouts a week, lifts below 70% were 52% of volume. [snippet] same source.
- Frequency: 4 to 6 sessions per week for developing lifters, doubles for elites. [believe]
- Deload: built into monthly waves (a lighter fourth week is the usual reading). [believe]
- **Who:** developing lifters with a coach and years ahead. **Equipment:** platform, bar, bumpers, racks, blocks. **Evidence:** COACH, built on large observational datasets of Soviet lifters; not RCTs. Note the Sportivny Press translation on how performance enhancers changed volume/intensity structure: the historical numbers are confounded by doping. [snippet] [Sportivny Press](https://www.sportivnypress.com/?p=303)

### 1.4 Bulgarian method (Ivan Abadjiev)

- **Prescribes:** a tiny exercise pool (snatch, clean and jerk, front squat, plus power variants; back squat inclusion is disputed), and working up to a **daily maximum**, which is the day's max, not the competition PR. Back off sets come as absolute drops (5 to 20 kg), not percentages. [snippet] [The Barbell](https://thebarbell.com/bulgarian-method/), [Fitness Volt](https://fitnessvolt.com/bulgarian-squat-method/)
- Frequency: up to 8 or more sessions a week at elite level, multiple sessions a day, a sample day being front squat, then snatch, then clean and jerk with breaks. [snippet] [Grinder Gym](https://grindergym.com/soviet-and-bulgarian-weightlifting-methods), [FloElite, Max Aita](https://www.floelite.com/articles/5050881-max-aita-explains-the-bulgarian-system)
- Disputed: some coaches argue there was never one "Bulgarian method", and lifters were not literally maxing several times daily. [snippet] [StrongFirst forum](https://www.strongfirst.com/community/threads/bulgarian-weightlifting.2262/latest)
- **Who:** selected, full time, technically mature elites. Not for novices or recreational adults. **Evidence:** COACH / anecdote; heavy doping context in the era. [believe]

### 1.5 Chinese approach

- **Prescribes:** classic lifts plus a large amount of strength work (squats, pulls, presses) and bodybuilding style accessory work for the upper back, trunk and arms; often framed as "weakness correction" around a base of front squat, back squat and snatch balance. [snippet] [All Things Gym](https://allthingsgym.com/chinese-weightlifting-training-methods), [Sportivny Press, Team China](https://www.sportivnypress.com/?p=7901)
- Squat frequency is **disputed**: one source says about twice a week, another says 3 to 4 sessions with squats. [snippet] [BoxLife](https://boxlifemagazine.com/chinese-squat-science-weightlifting-variation-system/), [Torokhtiy](https://torokhtiy.com/blogs/guides/chinese-olympic-weightlifting)
- Hypertrophy and positional strength take a larger share than in Bulgarian work; more moderate reps (3 to 5, sometimes 8 to 10 on accessories). [believe]
- **Who:** intermediate and advanced lifters with time for 5 to 6 sessions a week. **Evidence:** COACH, mostly second hand accounts from seminars and blog translations.

### 1.6 Catalyst Athletics (Greg Everett)

- Published programs on TrainHeroic, graded by level. **Level 1:** for athletes who have recently learned the snatch and clean and jerk, 4 days a week. **Level 2:** follows Level 1, loads set by feel rather than percentages, sample week Mon/Wed/Fri. **"Simplest Weightlifting Program in the World":** 4 weeks, 3 days a week, for beginners or people combining weightlifting with other training. Every session carries a complete warm up with weightlifting specific mobility. [snippet] [TrainHeroic, Level 1](https://marketplace.trainheroic.com/workout-plan/program/everett-program-1615930499), [Level 2](https://marketplace.trainheroic.com/workout-plan/program/everett-program-1615935537), [Simplest program](https://marketplace.trainheroic.com/workout-plan/program/everett-program-1475690651)
- Everett's selection rule: an advanced program will not make you advanced and is likely less effective than a beginner one. [snippet] [Catalyst, Choosing the right program](https://mail.catalystathletics.com/article/1908/Choosing-the-Right-Weightlifting-Program-And-Making-it-Work), [Program selection guide](https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2252/Guide-to-Catalyst-Athletics-Training-Program-Selection/)
- Catalyst places the Bulgarian approach at the "classic lifts and squats only" end of a spectrum and powerlifting influenced high volume programs at the other. [snippet] [Catalyst, Role of strength](https://catalystathletics.com/article/70/The-Role-of-Strength-in-Weightlifting)
- Typical structure (book, *Olympic Weightlifting: A Complete Guide for Athletes and Coaches*): snatch or clean and jerk variant first, then a pull, then a squat, then optional accessory; 4 week blocks with a lighter week. [believe]
- Full session content is behind the paid program; only the listing structure is public. **Evidence:** COACH.

### 1.7 USAW coaching basics and how a novice learns the lifts

- **Top down teaching:** start from the high hang/power position and work down to the floor. Harvey Newton's lesson sequence (SportsEdTV) starts with a high pull from the power position, then high hang power snatch. [snippet] [SportsEdTV lesson 3](https://sportsedtv.com/sport/weightlifting-instructional-videos/learn-to-snatch-lesson-3-snatch-pull-high-hang-no-blocks), [lesson 4](https://sportsedtv.com/sport/weightlifting-instructional-videos/learn-to-snatch-lesson-4-power-snatch-high-hang-no-blocks)
- **PVC first:** set grip width, then behind the neck snatch grip push press, then overhead squat as a mobility and stability check, then move to a barbell and rarely return to PVC except for remediation. [snippet] [TeamBuildr](https://blog.teambuildr.com/posts/coaching-olympic-lifting-high-schoolers)
- **Hang vs blocks:** coaches disagree. Blocks prevent cheating the pull and remove grip/postural fatigue between reps; the hang needs no equipment. [snippet] [SimpliFaster](https://simplifaster.com/?p=7046), [Torokhtiy, basic snatch progression](https://torokhtiy.com/blogs/guides/basic-snatch-progression)
- Burgener warm up (Mike Burgener, used in CrossFit and USAW circles): down and up, elbows high and outside, muscle snatch, snatch lands, hip to drop snatch, all with PVC or empty bar. [believe]. Could not confirm Catalyst uses it.
- I found no official usaweightlifting.org teaching document; an NSCA 2022 session claims to follow USAW best practice but its content was not visible. [snippet] [NSCA](https://www.nsca.com/events/videos/2022/2022-national-conference/fri1400swisher/)
- **Coaching need:** technique is the limiter, and video review at minimum, a coach at best, is the consensus. **Evidence:** COACH.

## 2 · CrossFit and functional fitness

### 2.1 CrossFit methodology (Greg Glassman)

- **Definition:** "constantly varied, high intensity, functional movement". [believe] (the phrase is CrossFit's own tagline, not re verified this session)
- **"What Is Fitness?"**, CrossFit Journal, October 2002. Ten general physical skills: cardiovascular/respiratory endurance, stamina, strength, flexibility, power, speed, coordination, agility, balance, accuracy. Three metabolic pathways: phosphagen (under about 10 s), glycolytic (up to several minutes), oxidative. [snippet] [CrossFit Games, What is fitness](https://games.crossfit.com/article/what-fitness). Fitness is also defined in the article as work capacity across broad time and modal domains, and by three standards (ten skills, hopper, pathways). [believe]
- **3 on 1 off template:** "A Theoretical Template for CrossFit's Programming", CrossFit Journal, February 2003. Days 1, 5, 9 single modality; 2, 6, 10 two modalities; 3, 7, 11 three modalities; modalities are M (monostructural cardio), G (gymnastics/bodyweight), W (weightlifting). The article itself notes the drawback that 3 on 1 off does not fit a gym's 5 on 2 off week. [snippet] [CrossFit Games, Theoretical template](https://games.crossfit.com/article/theoretical-template-crossfits-programming), [PDF reprint](https://library.crossfit.com/free/pdf/06_03_CF_Template.pdf)
- **Class structure** at affiliates: warm up and mobility (10 to 15 min) → skill or strength (15 to 20 min) → metcon (5 to 20 min, sometimes longer) → cool down or accessory. [believe] Search did not confirm an official template.
- **Scaling tiers:** Rx (as prescribed), Scaled (some experience), Foundations (newcomers). Scaling preserves the intended stimulus; order commonly taught is load, then reps, then movement. [snippet] [functionalfitness.pages.dev, How to read a WOD](https://functionalfitness.pages.dev/journal/how-to-read-a-wod/), [Breaking Muscle, The Rx factor](https://breakingmuscle.com/the-rx-factor-3-reasons-why-you-must-scale-your-wods/)
- **Benchmarks:** the "Girls" (Fran: 21-15-9 thrusters 43/30 kg and pull ups; Grace: 30 clean and jerks 61/43 kg for time; Helen: 3 rounds 400 m run, 21 kettlebell swings 24/16 kg, 12 pull ups) and Hero WODs (Murph: 1 mile run, 100 pull ups, 200 push ups, 300 squats, 1 mile run, with 9/6 kg vest). [believe] for the exact loads. Benchmarks only count if movement standards stay constant between attempts. [snippet] [RitFit benchmarks](https://www.ritfitsports.com/blogs/article/the-ultimate-guide-to-crossfit-benchmark-workouts-standards-progress-and-performance)
- **Testing:** retest benchmarks every 8 to 12 weeks, plus 1RM lifts and the annual Open (5 week online test). [believe]
- **Who:** general population who want broad fitness and a group setting. **Equipment:** barbell, rig, rings, rower/bike/ski, kettlebells, wall balls, boxes. **Evidence:** COACH. Whether "constant variation" outperforms planned periodization is not established by RCTs. [believe]

### 2.2 Injury evidence

| study | design | rate |
|:-|:-|:-|
| Hak, Hodzovic, Hickey 2013, J Strength Cond Res | online survey, n=132, 186 injuries | **3.1 per 1000 h** [snippet] [PubMed 24276294](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24276294/) |
| Weisenthal et al. 2014, Orthop J Sports Med | survey, n=386 | original paper reported **prevalence** (about 19.4% injured [believe]); authors' later reply computed **2.4 per 1000 h** from 36,928.7 exposure hours and 89 injuries [snippet] [OJSM](https://sage.cnpereading.com/doi/10.1177/2325967114543254) |
| Montalvo et al. 2017, J Sports Sci Med | cohort | 2.3 per 1000 h [snippet] [JSSM](https://www.jssm.org/jssm-16-53.xml-Fulltext) |
| Klimek et al. 2018, J Sport Rehabil 27:295-299 | systematic review | concluded CrossFit injury rates are comparable to, or lower than, other common exercise and strength training [snippet, via BMJ OSEM summary]; its own per 1000 h range not confirmed (I believe roughly 0.27 to 3.3) [believe] [DOI 10.1123/jsr.2016-0040](https://doi.org/10.1123/jsr.2016-0040) |
| Rodríguez et al. 2021 (Phys Sportsmed), systematic review | review | prevalence 35.3%, incidence 0.2 to 18.9 per 1000 h [snippet] [T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/10.1080/00913847.2020.1864675) |
| HIFT meta-analysis 2018 | meta-analysis | prevalence 36%, 4.29 per 1000 h [snippet] |
| Dominski et al. | systematic review | 1.94 to 3.1 per 1000 h [snippet] |

Most injured regions across these studies: shoulder first, then lower back, then knee. [believe] Shoulder and low back are consistently top two in the reviews I remember; not re confirmed this session. Evidence: **OBS** (self report surveys, recall bias, no consistent injury definition) synthesised in **META**. Bottom line: CrossFit is in the same range as recreational weight training and running, roughly 2 to 4 per 1000 h.

### 3.2 HYROX training plans

- No official HYROX or Roxfit plan surfaced in search; the official training ecosystem (HYROX365 / Roxfit gyms and the official app) is [believe] and unverified.

### 3.3 Alex Viada (The Hybrid Athlete, Complete Human Performance)

- **Principles:** minimum effective volume for each quality; do not stack a full powerlifting plan on a full marathon plan; cut overlapping modalities; keep easy aerobic work truly easy (do not "power through" Zone 2); manage fatigue and recovery first. [snippet] (secondary write ups of his lectures and podcasts) [Tony Gentilcore](https://tonygentilcore.com/tag/hybrid-athlete/), [Polar, Hybrid athlete blueprint](https://www.polar.com/en/journal/hybrid-athlete), [Victory Belt, The Ultimate Hybrid Athlete](https://www.victorybelt.com/ultimate-hybrid-athlete)
- Also [believe]: prioritise one quality per block, separate hard lower body lifting from hard running by time or by day, put the priority session first in the day, use cycling or low impact cardio when leg strength is the priority. A "never within 6 h" rule appeared in a 2026 article but not attributed to Viada. [snippet]
- **Evidence:** COACH, consistent with the meta-analyses below.

### 3.6 Concurrent training interference: the evidence

| paper | design | finding |
|:-|:-|:-|
| Wilson et al. 2012, J Strength Cond Res | META, 21 studies, 422 ES | ES strength only vs concurrent: hypertrophy 1.23 vs 0.85, strength 1.76 vs 1.44, **power 0.91 vs 0.55**. Running, not cycling, decreased hypertrophy and strength; interference rose with endurance frequency and duration [last point: believe]. [snippet] [BVS record](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/sms/resource/pt/mdl-22002517) |
| Petré et al. 2021, Sports Med 51:991-1010 | META, 27 studies, lower body 1RM | trained individuals lose some 1RM gain (ES -0.35); moderately trained (-0.20, ns) and untrained (0.03) do not; the effect only appeared when both were in the **same session**. [snippet] [PMC8053170 PDF](https://rcastoragev2.blob.core.windows.net/9b3367f4a0e756e8ddc4409d23c93c6b/PMC8053170.pdf) |
| Schumann et al. 2022, Sports Med 52:601-612 | META, 43 studies, n=1090 | **no compromise of hypertrophy or maximal strength**; **explosive strength attenuated**, mainly when done in the same session (p=0.043) vs separated by at least 3 h (ns); not moderated by aerobic mode, frequency, training status or age. [snippet] [PubMed 34757594](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34757594/), [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-021-01587-7) |
| Lundberg et al. 2022, Sports Med 52:2391-2403 | META, fibre size | type I fibre growth blunted when aerobic work was running, not cycling (preliminary). [snippet] |

Reading for the agent: for most people, adding cardio does not cost muscle or max strength. Power and, in already trained lifters, some max strength suffer when both are crammed into one session. Separate by 3 h or more, or by day; prefer cycling when leg hypertrophy is the goal. Evidence: **META**, the strongest in this whole cluster.

## 4 · For the agent

1. **Gate Olympic lifts behind a technique check.** Do not program snatch or clean and jerk from the floor unless the user reports coached instruction or submits video. Otherwise teach top down with PVC (grip, snatch grip push press, overhead squat), then high hang, then hang or blocks, and substitute power variants, pulls and front squats. Source: SportsEdTV / Harvey Newton, TeamBuildr, Catalyst program selection guide (an advanced program does not make you advanced).
2. **Never prescribe Bulgarian daily maxing to a non elite.** Default novice weightlifting to 3 to 4 sessions a week, classic lift variant then pull then squat, most work at 70 to 85%. Source: Catalyst Level 1 and Simplest program, Medvedyev summaries.
3. **Cap classic lift volume with Prilepin.** Per exercise per session: 70-80% → 3-6 reps per set, about 18 total; 80-90% → 2-4, about 15; 90%+ → 1-2, 4 to 10 total. Source: Prilepin's chart (EliteFTS, Lift Vault).
4. **Scale CrossFit style workouts to preserve stimulus, in this order: load, then reps, then movement,** and assign Rx / Scaled / Foundations from the interview. Keep a benchmark (for example a Fran or Helen equivalent) retested on an 8 to 12 week cycle with identical standards. Source: CrossFit Rx/Scaled/Foundations explainers, RitFit benchmarks.
5. **Use the 3 on 1 off M/G/W rotation only when the user's week allows it;** for a 5 day schedule, rotate single, double and triple modality days within 5 on 2 off, as the 2003 template itself admits the 3/1 pattern does not fit gym weeks. Source: CrossFit Journal, Theoretical Template (Feb 2003).
6. **Separate heavy legs from hard running.** Same day only if at least 3 h apart and with the priority session first; better on separate days. Put hard intervals and heavy squats/sleds at least 24 h apart when possible. For trained lifters chasing max strength or power, never stack them in one session. Source: Schumann 2022 (≥3 h removes the explosive strength penalty), Petré 2021 (same session penalty in trained people).
7. **When leg hypertrophy is a goal, prefer bike or rower over running for extra conditioning.** Source: Wilson 2012, Lundberg 2022 (Schumann 2022 found no mode effect for strength, so treat this as a low cost hedge, not a law).
8. **HYROX 4 day week template for a beginner who can run 30 min:** Day 1 easy run (zone 2, conversational); Day 2 lower body strength (squat, lunge, sled or heavy push) plus short station practice; Day 3 rest or easy bike; Day 4 interval run (for example 6 to 8 x 1 km at goal race pace); Day 5 rest; Day 6 HYROX simulation or compromised running (1 km run + station, 4 to 8 rounds), added weekly from about week 5; Day 7 rest. Heavy legs (Day 2) sit 48 h from the intervals. Train at least 2 to 3 times on the real sled, SkiErg and wall ball target before race day. Source: Men's Fitness AU 8 week plan, Centr beginner plan, Genesis Fitness, Schumann 2022.
9. **Hard code HYROX station loads from a versioned table, keyed by season and division,** and remind the user to confirm with the current rulebook; never interpolate a weight. Source: Red Bull, Roxlyfe, Fitness Volt (sources disagree on wall ball target heights and one outlier on Women Open farmers).
