# 04 · General health, fat loss, special populations, safety and load finding

Research cluster for the shareable "Personal trainer" agent. Scope: what the public health bodies prescribe, what exercise does and does not do for fat loss, how beginners, older adults and women should be handled, how to screen and route to a professional, and how to find a safe working load for someone with no numbers. The agent gives no medical advice: everything below is framed as "general exercise guidance, routed to a professional when a flag appears".

### 1.2 UK Chief Medical Officers 2019

- Adults: strength based activity at least 2 days/week, framed as slowing the loss of muscle and bone that begins around 50 [checked]. Aerobic: 150 min moderate or 75 min vigorous or a mix, and it can be accumulated in short bouts [believe; the "any bout length counts" point appears in coverage, "even a few minutes is good for you"].
- Older adults: activities for strength, balance and flexibility at least 2 days/week; examples dancing, bowls, Tai Chi [checked]. Daily activity, and "any activity is better than none", even light [checked].
- Everyone: minimise sedentary time and break up long sitting with at least light activity [checked].
- The 2019 report added bone strengthening explicitly [checked, via Royal Osteoporosis Society news].
- Evidence level: guideline.

### 1.4 ACSM 2009 position stand: Progression models in resistance training (Med Sci Sports Exerc 41(3):687 to 708)

- Novice (no RT experience or not trained for several years): loads corresponding to an **8 to 12 RM** range [checked].
- Frequency: **2 to 3 days/week** for novice and intermediate, total body for untrained [checked]. Intermediate split routines 3 to 4 days; advanced 4 to 6 [believe].
- Progression rule: **increase load 2 to 10% when the person can perform the current workload for 1 to 2 reps over the desired number** [checked]. Smaller increments (about 2 to 5%) for upper body and small muscle groups, larger (5 to 10%) for lower body [believe].
- Sets: novices 1 to 3 sets per exercise [believe]. Rest: 2 to 3 min for multi joint core lifts at heavier loads, 1 to 2 min for assistance work [checked for 2 to 3 min; believe for the rest].
- Order: large before small muscle groups, multi joint before single joint [believe].
- Older adults: same principles plus power training at light to moderate loads (30 to 60% 1RM) moved fast [believe].
- Note: ExRx lists a 2026 ACSM update ("Resistance Training Prescription for Muscle Function, Hypertrophy, and Physical Performance in Healthy Adults") that supersedes the 2009 stand [checked that it exists, content not read]. **Action: the next research pass should read the 2026 ACSM document**, because the agent should cite the current stand.
- Evidence level: position stand, graded A to D per statement.
Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19204579/ · https://exrx.net/WeightTraining/Guidelines.html

## 2 · Fat loss: what exercise contributes

| claim | detail | mark | level |
|:-|:-|:-|:-|
| Exercise alone produces modest weight loss | Aerobic programmes at public health doses give up to about 2 kg, with large individual variation; clinically significant loss is unlikely unless volume is very high (Swift et al. 2014, Prog Cardiovasc Dis 56(4):441 to 447) | [checked] | narrative review of RCTs |
| Exercise is key for keeping weight off | Exercise plays an important role in preventing regain after loss (Swift 2014); health benefits occur even without weight loss, set realistic expectations | [checked] | review |
| Dose (ACSM, Donnelly et al. 2009, MSSE 41(2):459 to 471) | 150 to 250 min/week moderate prevents weight gain and gives modest loss; more than 250 min/week is associated with clinically significant loss and helps maintenance after loss; with moderate diet restriction, 150 to 250 min/week improves loss | [checked] | position stand |
| Resistance training alone is not effective for weight loss but improves body composition and health markers | Donnelly 2009 | [believe] | position stand |
| RT preserves lean mass in a deficit, older obese adults | Sardeli et al. 2018 (Nutrients): RT offset about 93.5% of the lean mass lost with caloric restriction, 6 RCTs | [checked] | SR/MA, small |
| Nuance across ages | Lopez et al. 2022 (MSSE), 65 trials, n = 2537: RT alone +0.8 kg lean mass; RT combined with caloric restriction still showed a significant lean mass reduction (attenuated, not abolished) | [checked] | SR/MA |
| NEAT and steps | Non exercise activity thermogenesis varies by hundreds of kcal/day between people (Levine) [believe]. Steps: Paluch et al. 2022 (Lancet Public Health) found mortality risk falls with more steps, plateauing around 6,000 to 8,000/day for 60+ and 8,000 to 10,000 for younger adults [believe, search returned nothing] | [believe] | cohort MA |

**Principle the agent can state, without writing a nutrition plan:** fat loss is driven mainly by a sustained energy deficit, which is a dietary matter; exercise adds a modest amount, is strongly linked to keeping lost weight off, and resistance training plus adequate protein helps keep muscle while dieting. Writing calorie targets or meal plans crosses into dietetics (a regulated profession in many countries), and for anyone with a medical condition, an eating disorder history, pregnancy or medication, it belongs to a doctor or registered dietitian. The agent routes, it does not prescribe food.

Sources: https://repository.lsu.edu/kinesiology_pubs/262 · https://scholarlywork.ochsner.org/display/c9058394 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19127177/ · https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/10/4/423 · https://ro.ecu.edu.au/ecuworks2022-2026/1500


## 3 · Beginners

What established novice programmes share (Starting Strength, StrongLifts, GZCLP, the ACSM novice model, NSCA beginner templates) [believe for the programme list, checked for the ACSM parts]:

1. **Full body, 2 to 3 sessions/week**, a day of rest between [checked, ACSM 2009].
2. **Few multi joint movements** (squat or leg press pattern, hinge, push, pull, carry), 1 to 3 sets [believe].
3. **8 to 12 RM style loads** that are not taken to failure early on [checked for 8 to 12 RM].
4. **Linear progression**: add a little load whenever the target reps are beaten by 1 to 2 (2 to 10%) [checked]. Novices can progress session to session for weeks to months [believe].
5. Technique first: the first weeks are partly neural learning, so strength rises faster than muscle size [believe].

Adherence: dropout from new exercise programmes is commonly cited around 50% within 6 months (Dishman) [believe]. Predictors of adherence include enjoyment, self efficacy, low initial burden and supervision or social support [believe]. Practical implication: start with short sessions (30 to 45 min), 2 to 3 days, and a programme the person actually likes; "minimum effective dose" beats an ambitious plan they abandon [believe].

## 6 · Safety and screening

### 6.1 PAR-Q+ (PAR-Q+ Collaboration, current form copyright 2023, template dated 01-11-2022)

Structure [checked]: page 1 has **7 general health questions**. NO to all 7: cleared to become more active, start slowly and build up. YES to any one: **not** an automatic referral, the person completes the follow-up questions on pages 2 and 3 (condition specific), and if any follow-up is YES, they are routed to a qualified exercise professional and/or the ePARmed-X+ online tool, or to their doctor.

The 7 questions, wording from memory of the official form [believe, only Q1 confirmed]:

1. Has your doctor ever said that you have a heart condition OR high blood pressure? [checked]
2. Do you feel pain in your chest at rest, during daily activities, OR when you do physical activity?
3. Do you lose balance because of dizziness OR have you lost consciousness in the last 12 months? (dizziness from hard breathing during vigorous exercise excepted)
4. Have you ever been diagnosed with another chronic medical condition (other than heart disease or high blood pressure)?
5. Are you currently taking prescribed medications for a chronic medical condition?
6. Do you currently have (or have had within the past 12 months) a bone, joint or soft tissue problem that could be made worse by becoming more active?
7. Has your doctor ever said that you should only do medically supervised physical activity?

Follow-up sections cover (from memory) arthritis/osteoporosis/back problems, cancer, heart or cardiovascular conditions, high blood pressure, metabolic conditions (diabetes), mental health and learning disability, respiratory disease, spinal cord injury, stroke, other conditions [believe].
Delay becoming more active if [believe]: you have a temporary illness such as a cold or fever; you are pregnant (talk to your health care practitioner or complete the ePARmed-X+); your health changes (answer the questionnaire again).

Sources: https://vancouver.ca/files/cov/parq-plus.pdf · https://hfjc.library.ubc.ca/index.php/HFJC/article/download/839/422/2085 · https://eparmedx.com (official, not opened)

### 6.2 ACSM preparticipation screening algorithm (Riebe et al. 2015, MSSE 47(11):2473 to 2479)

Three inputs [checked]: (1) current physical activity level, (2) known cardiovascular, metabolic or renal disease, or signs and symptoms suggestive of it, (3) desired exercise intensity. Risk factor counting was removed [checked]. "Exerciser" = planned structured activity of at least moderate intensity, 30 min, 3 days/week, for the past 3 months [checked]. Goal is to remove unnecessary barriers [checked].

Decision table [believe for the branch outputs, structure checked]:

| | no disease, no symptoms | known CV/metabolic/renal disease, no symptoms | any signs or symptoms |
|:-|:-|:-|:-|
| **not currently exercising** | no clearance; start light to moderate, progress gradually | medical clearance recommended; then light to moderate | medical clearance recommended |
| **currently exercising** | no clearance; continue moderate or progress to vigorous | moderate: continue; vigorous: clearance recommended | stop exercising, seek medical clearance |

Signs and symptoms list (ACSM major signs) [believe]: pain or discomfort in the chest, neck, jaw or arms possibly from ischaemia; shortness of breath at rest or with mild exertion; dizziness or syncope; orthopnoea or paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea; ankle oedema; palpitations or tachycardia; intermittent claudication; known heart murmur; unusual fatigue or breathlessness with usual activities.
Whitfield (NHANES) found the 2015 algorithm referred fewer adults than earlier screens [checked].
Sources: https://digitalcommons.uri.edu/kinesiology_facpubs/180 · https://www.ideafit.com/getting-to-the-heart-of-pre-exercise-screening · https://nfpt.com/assessing-cardiovascular-risk-with-fitness-clients/

### 6.3 Red flags: stop the session and advise medical help [believe; these are standard ACSM/NHS stop criteria]

Chest pain or pressure; pain spreading to arm, jaw or neck; breathlessness out of proportion to effort or not settling with rest; dizziness, fainting or near fainting; irregular or racing heartbeat; sudden severe headache; sudden weakness, numbness, slurred speech or facial droop (call emergency services); calf pain and swelling; in pregnancy the ACOG list above. Sudden severe pain, a pop with swelling, numbness or tingling down a limb, loss of bladder or bowel control or saddle numbness with back pain (emergency), night pain or unexplained weight loss with pain.

### 6.4 Injury handling without diagnosing [believe; general practice consensus]

- Never name a diagnosis or a cause. Describe the situation back ("you report knee pain on stairs") and route.
- Pain rule commonly used in physio practice: pain during exercise up to about 3 or 4 out of 10 that settles within 24 h is usually acceptable for non serious, already assessed problems; anything sharper, worsening, or still elevated next day means reduce or stop and see a professional.
- Agent behaviour: substitute the painful movement with a pain free alternative, lower the load or range, and recommend a physiotherapist or doctor for anything persistent beyond about 1 to 2 weeks, recurring, or following trauma.


## 7 · Load finding for a newcomer with no numbers

| tool | evidence | mark |
|:-|:-|:-|
| RIR accuracy (Halperin et al. 2022, Sports Med, scoping review and exploratory MA) | 12 studies, 414 participants; people **underpredict reps to failure by about 0.95 reps** (CI 0.17 to 1.73), very high heterogeneity; predictions more accurate **closer to failure** and in **lower rep sets**; training status had no meaningful effect; whether this error is acceptable is unclear | [checked] |
| Coaches judging others | absolute errors about 4.8, 2.0 and 1.2 reps at 33%, 66% and 90% of a set (Emanuel, Halperin, preprint 2022) | [checked] |
| Practical implication | early in a set and at high reps everyone, novice or not, is poor at RIR; ask for an RIR call at the END of the set; novices should aim for a conservative "2 to 4 RIR" | inferred from above |
| Rep based 1RM estimates | Epley: 1RM = w × (1 + reps/30). Brzycki: 1RM = w × 36 / (37 − reps). Both are reasonable only for sets of about 10 reps or fewer and diverge above that | [believe] |
| NSCA %1RM table | see 1.5, point estimate | [checked] |
| Autoregulation for strength (Zhang et al. 2021, Front Physiol) | 8 studies, 166 participants: autoregulated training gave larger maximal strength gains than fixed loading (ES 0.64), APRE largest (0.78); small sample, trained subjects | [checked] |
| Autoregulation (Hickmott et al. 2022, Sports Med Open) | RIR based RPE and velocity based load prescription gave **similar** strength gains to standardised prescription | [checked] |
| Network MA 2025 (Zhang Y et al., J Exerc Sci Fit) | APRE, RPE and VBT above percentage based for max strength; seen only secondhand | [checked that it exists, numbers unverified] |
| Readiness questionnaires (Hooper index, Hooper and Mackinnon 1995) | sum of 1 to 7 ratings of sleep quality, fatigue, stress and muscle soreness; used in sport to track readiness; validity in recreational populations is modest | [believe] |

**Conservative first session protocol** (synthesis of ACSM novice loads, NSCA warm up practice and the RIR evidence) [believe as a protocol, components cited]:

1. Screen first (PAR-Q+ style, section 6). No session before a clean screen.
2. Warm up 5 to 10 min of light aerobic work, then movement rehearsal with no load or a very light implement.
3. For each exercise, pick a load the person is confident they could lift 15 or more times. Do 8 to 10 reps.
4. After each set, ask for RIR at the end ("how many more could you have done with good form?"). Ramp in small steps (about 5 to 10%) across 2 to 4 sets until a set of 10 lands at roughly **3 to 4 RIR**, or form changes, or RPE about 6 to 7 out of 10. Stop there: that is the working load for session 2.
5. Never test a 1RM in week 1. If an estimate is needed, use Epley or Brzycki on a submaximal set of 10 or fewer reps.
6. Keep total volume low in session 1 (about 2 working sets per exercise, 4 to 6 exercises) to limit next day soreness, the most common reason novices quit [believe].
7. From session 2: linear progression by the 2 to 10% rule when target reps are beaten by 1 to 2 (ACSM 2009), moving toward 1 to 3 RIR over the first weeks.
8. Daily readiness check (sleep, soreness, stress, energy, pain, 1 to 5 each). A poor score or a new pain reduces the day's load or volume rather than cancelling; a red flag stops the session.


## For the agent

Rules ready to encode. Each carries its source and mark.

1. **Screening gate before any programme.** Ask the 7 PAR-Q+ general questions (section 6.1). All NO: proceed. Any YES: do not build a programme yet; tell the person to complete the official PAR-Q+ follow-up and the ePARmed-X+ (eparmedx.com) or speak to their doctor or a qualified exercise professional, and resume only when they report clearance. Source: PAR-Q+ 2023 structure [checked], wording [believe].
2. **ACSM disease and symptom check.** Separately ask about known heart, metabolic (diabetes) or kidney disease and the ACSM symptoms list. Symptoms at all, or known disease plus being currently inactive, or known disease plus wanting vigorous training: recommend medical clearance first. Source: Riebe 2015 [checked structure, believe branch outputs].
3. **Hard stops at any time.** Chest pain or pressure, fainting or near fainting, unusual breathlessness, palpitations, stroke signs, calf pain with swelling, or in pregnancy any ACOG warning sign: the agent stops the session and tells the person to seek medical care (emergency services for chest pain or stroke signs). Source: ACOG 804 [checked partially], ACSM stop criteria [believe].
4. **Default general health prescription** (no goal beyond health): 150 to 300 min/week moderate aerobic or 75 to 150 vigorous, plus full body strength 2 or more days/week, plus less sitting. Source: WHO 2020 [checked], UK CMO 2019 [checked].
5. **Beginner default.** Full body, 2 to 3 non consecutive days/week, 4 to 6 multi joint exercises, 1 to 3 sets in the 8 to 12 rep range, add 2 to 10% when target reps are beaten by 1 to 2. Source: ACSM 2009 [checked].
6. **Safe first session protocol.** No 1RM testing; start with a load liftable 15+ times, ramp in small steps until a set of 10 ends at about 3 to 4 RIR, ask RIR only at the end of the set, cap volume at about 2 working sets per exercise. Source: Halperin 2022 [checked] for RIR behaviour, protocol [believe].
7. **Load estimation.** If a number is needed, estimate 1RM from a submaximal set of 10 or fewer reps with Epley or Brzycki, and treat the NSCA %1RM table as a starting guess, then autoregulate by RIR. Source: NSCA table [checked], formulas [believe], autoregulation equivalent or better than fixed % [checked, Zhang 2021, Hickmott 2022].
8. **65+ rules.** Always include balance and functional exercise, aim for 3 days/week of multicomponent work, include strength and some fast intent (power) work at light to moderate loads, and recommend a health professional led falls programme for anyone who has fallen or fears falling. Source: WHO 2020 [checked], Sherrington 2019 [checked], power MA [checked].
9. **Low bone density.** Do not prescribe heavy or impact loading unsupervised; state that supervised high intensity resistance and impact training (LIFTMOR) improved bone density and suggest doing it with a physio or qualified coach after medical sign off. Source: Watson 2018 [checked].
10. **Pregnancy.** If pregnant: no programme until they confirm their obstetric provider has no objection; then aim for 150 min/week moderate activity plus strength, continue prior activity rather than start vigorous new work, avoid contact, fall risk, scuba and long supine lying after the first trimester, and stop on any ACOG warning sign. Postpartum: resume gradually once their provider clears it. Source: ACOG 804 [checked partially], WHO 2020 [checked].
11. **Women and the cycle.** Programme women with the same principles as men; do not plan by cycle phase by default, adjust day to day by readiness. Source: Roberts 2020 [checked], McNulty 2020 [checked conclusion], Colenso-Semple 2023 [checked].
12. **Fat loss scope.** State the principle (energy deficit is mainly diet; exercise adds modestly, matters most for keeping weight off; strength training helps retain muscle while dieting; more than 250 min/week of activity for meaningful exercise driven loss) and never write calorie targets or meal plans; refer to a doctor or registered dietitian. Source: Swift 2014 [checked], Donnelly 2009 [checked], Sardeli 2018 and Lopez 2022 [checked].
13. **Pain without diagnosis.** Never name a diagnosis. Substitute a pain free movement, lower load or range; mild pain (about 3 out of 10 or less) that settles within 24 h may be trained around; worsening, sharp, traumatic or persistent pain beyond 1 to 2 weeks goes to a physiotherapist or doctor.
