# 07 · The coach journey audit · how a great coach takes on and keeps a client

Research note for the shareable "Personal trainer" agent. Compiled 2026-11-13. Written from the seat of a strength and conditioning coach (NSCA-CSCS standard of practice) who has coached beginners, older adults, runners, team-sport athletes, physique clients and people coming back from injury.

**Scope.** The whole journey from first contact to month six, stage by stage. **Not covered here:** the wording and branching of the onboarding questions, which `06_interview_audit.md` owns (the fork, outcome first, experience by domain, pain as status, styles, recovery block, sport and events, barriers). Where a stage below depends on a 06 change it says so and does not repeat it.

**What was read.** `CLAUDE.md`, `TRAINER.md`, `docs/HOW_IT_WORKS.md`, `doctrine/00` to `08`, `.claude/skills/train/SKILL.md`, `.claude/agents/cycle-auditor.md`, the owner's walkthrough notes, the `STR` table in `site/app.js`, and the engine where a claim depended on it: `engine/build.py` (`refill`, `pick_session`, `build`, the `PRIORITY` table), `engine/match.py` (`filters`, `score`), `engine/logbook.py` (`advance`, `deload_triggers`), `engine/plan.py` (`cmd_week`), and the `layouts` of `hyrox-prep-4` and `muscle-upperlower-4`.

## How to read the marks

- **[checked]** confirmed this session in an abstract, a PubMed or repository record, or an official page, as returned by web search. No full paper was opened.

- **[believe]** coaching practice or memory, not re-checked. A lead, not a rule.

## Part 1 · The evidence a coach stands on

| claim | what it means for the agent | mark · sources |
|:-|:-|:-|
| **About half of people who start an exercise programme drop out within six months, most of them in the first 12 weeks.** | Retention is the outcome. A perfect program that is quit in week 7 scores zero. The first 12 weeks deserve their own design. | [seen] Dishman's reviews via [synapsesocial record](https://synapsesocial.com/papers/W2114032204) and [BISp record of Dishman 1987](https://www.bisp-surf.de/Record/PU198705029833) · Stiggelbout et al. citing Dishman, [typeset PDF](https://typeset.io/pdf/dropout-from-exercise-programs-for-seniors-a-prospective-2xw080hjwo.pdf) · [Boise State review](https://experts.boisestate.edu/en/publications/factors-related-to-physical-activity-adherence-in-women-review-an/). Caveat: mostly younger adults; home programmes can do better. |
| **Supervision changes results, not just adherence.** Trained men with a personal trainer progressed loads faster and gained more 1RM strength over 12 weeks than the same program unsupervised; both improved. | The value of a coach is partly the load decision each session. The agent already owns loads in code; what it lacks is the human loop around them (check-ins, wins, re-tests). | [checked] Mazzetti 2000, MSSE, [PubMed 10862549](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10862549/) (n = 20, small) · [seen] Coleman 2024 supervised vs unsupervised, via [Biolayne summary](https://biolayne.com/reps/issue-23/is-supervision-effective-for-gains/) |
| **Self-monitoring plus at least one other control-theory technique** (goal setting, feedback on performance, review of goals) beat other interventions (effect 0.42 vs 0.26); self-monitoring explained the most variance. | Logging alone is half the loop. The agent logs well; it rarely feeds the log back as **feedback against a goal** and never **reviews the goal**. | [checked] Michie 2009, Health Psychol, [PubMed 19916637](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19916637/) · [checked] 2017 review in overweight adults supporting goal setting plus self-monitoring, [IJBNPA](https://link.springer.com/10.1186/s12966-017-0494-y) |
| **Goal setting works for physical activity** (45 articles, 52 interventions) and multi-component goal setting (goal + feedback + review) is the stronger form. Rigid SMART targets have critics. | Outcome goals alone are weak. A coach sets an outcome, a process goal (sessions a week), and a performance goal (a number), then reviews them. | [checked] McEwan 2016, Health Psychol Rev, [Bath portal](https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/the-effectiveness-of-multi-component-goal-setting-interventions-f/) · [checked] the SMART critique, [UOW record](https://ro.uow.edu.au/test2021/4303) |
| **Planning when and where** (implementation intentions) raises physical activity, small to medium effect (d 0.31 after, 0.24 at follow-up), larger **when barrier planning is included**. | A weekly "which days, and what if X gets in the way" turn is the cheapest adherence lever there is. | [checked] Bélanger-Gravel 2013, [McMaster record](https://www.mcmasteroptimalaging.org/full-article/he/meta-analytic-review-effect-implementation-intentions-physical-activity-25322) · [checked] [PEDro record](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/36053) |
| **Autonomy and competence predict adherence**; intrinsic motivation predicts long-term adherence better than health or body motives. | People keep training when they feel capable and it is theirs. Visible wins (competence) and real choices (autonomy) are retention features, not decoration. | [checked] Teixeira 2012 SR, 66 studies, [IJBNPA PDF](https://ijbnpa.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/1479-5868-9-78) · [checked] [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/7f329e2f5a7f4f35bdd468a949a85a40). Mostly non-experimental. |
| **Habit takes weeks to months** (median about 66 days, range 18 to 254), and **missing one occasion did not materially derail it**; very inconsistent people did not form the habit. New gym members needed about four sessions a week for six weeks (file 06). | One missed session must never read as failure. A missed week is the danger zone; the re-entry matters more than the miss. | [seen] Lally 2010, via [UCL news](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2009/aug/how-long-does-it-take-form-habit) and [BPS digest](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/how-form-habit) · Kaushal & Rhodes 2015 [checked in file 06] |
| **The lapse is not the danger, the reaction to it is** (Marlatt's abstinence violation effect): guilt after a missed stretch turns a lapse into quitting. | Copy after a miss must be neutral and forward ("the next one is ready"), streak displays must not reset to zero in a way that shames, and the re-entry should be easy. | [seen] relapse prevention applied to exercise dropout, [PMC2829243](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2829243) · [seen] ACSM-CEP study guide on lapse after a missed week, [open-exam-prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/acsm-cep/education-behavior/adherence-relapse). Empirical support for the taxonomy is mixed. |
| **Short breaks cost little.** Strength is largely kept over short stops of up to about 3 to 4 weeks; detraining is "short-term" up to 4 weeks. | After a missed week the agent should not cut loads hard; after 2+ weeks a modest cut (it uses 15%) is reasonable. | [seen] Bosquet 2013 meta-analysis, [Scand J Med Sci Sports record](https://zendy.io/title/10.1111/sms.12047) · [seen] Mujika & Padilla 2000 via [Frontiers 2023](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fphys.2023.1334766/full). The exact week count differs between sources: [believe] for 3 vs 4. |
| **Periodisation helps strength modestly** (ES 0.43, 18 studies; 0.31 when volume is equated) and **not hypertrophy** once volume is equal. | Planned blocks and variation are worth having for strength goals; for muscle goals, weekly volume and effort matter more than the model. | [checked] Williams 2017, Sports Med, [PDF](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/05/Comparison-of-Periodized-and-Non-Periodized-Resistance.pdf) · [checked] Moesgaard 2022, [PubMed 35044672](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35044672/) |
| **Deloads are near universal in practice and barely studied.** Expert consensus treats them as a planned or reactive drop in volume or intensity; a one-week full stop at mid-block cost some lower-body strength and nothing else. | Keep the reduced-dose deload (the agent's half sets, 10% lighter, "4 left"). Never deload by stopping. | [checked] Bell 2023 Delphi, [Sports Med Open, DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/599784d71dff41b197ff5cf66a3d11dd) · [checked] Coleman 2024, [PeerJ 16777](https://peerj.com/articles/16777) |
| **Weekly volume per muscle has a dose response** (10+ hard sets per muscle a week beat fewer; diminishing returns above). | A muscle the client asks to bring up needs its weekly sets counted and raised, not one extra set somewhere. | [checked in file 01] Schoenfeld 2017 · Pelland 2025 |
| **Simple field tests are valid enough to track change.** The 30 s chair stand correlates with leg press strength (r 0.71 to 0.78), test-retest 0.84 to 0.92. The UKK 2 km walk predicts VO2max (r 0.7 to 0.85) and tracks training change (error 3.1 to 4.9 ml/kg/min). | A short, safe baseline battery is available for every population without a max test. | [checked] Jones 1999, [PubMed 10380242](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10380242/) · [seen] [Shirley Ryan AbilityLab summary](https://www.sralab.org/rehabilitation-measures/30-second-sit-stand-test) · [checked] UKK walk validity, [AGRIS](https://agris.fao.org/search/ru/records/65de62546eef00c2cea07b3b) and [Int J Sports Med 2000](https://www.thieme-connect.com/products/ejournals/abstract/10.1055/s-2000-8872). Push-up test norms (CSEP, ACSM) [believe], not found this session. |
| **NSCA and ACSM put assessment before program and re-assessment inside it**: health appraisal, then a fitness evaluation chosen from the needs analysis, goals set from both, tests repeated to judge the program. | The agent has the health appraisal (PAR-Q+) and nothing after it. | [checked in file 06] NSCA-CPT handbook and content outline · [believe] ACSM Guidelines 11th ed. (2021) structure; field-test chapters not confirmed this session, [ACSM](https://www.acsm.org/blog-detail/acsm-blog/2021/01/12/acsm-guidelines-for-exercise-testing-and-prescription-11th-edition-qa-senior-editor-gary-liguori) |

## Part 3 · The coach's eye · silent checks on every plan

A coach runs these in their head on every program and every session. Today the agent runs some in code (minutes, excluded regions, fillable slots) and some only at block end (sets per muscle, muscle goals only). Proposed: one function, `checks.coach_eye(plan, sessions, profile)`, run at `choose`, at every `build`, and on an adopted routine (06 Part 3b). It never changes a number by itself; it returns findings, and the doctrine says which ones block, which ones adjust, and which ones are said.

| # | check | rule of thumb | on fail | today |
|:-|:-|:-|:-|:-|
| 1 | **Days match** | sessions in the rotation equal days a week, or the plan says plainly how it runs | block the offer, or say "this plan's 4 sessions rotate over your 3 days, so each comes round every 9 to 10 days" | not checked [code] |
| 2 | **Weekly sets per muscle** | 10 to 20 hard sets for the goal's muscles (fractional counting), at the real frequency of their days | adjust accessory sets | at block end only, muscle goals only [code: `cycle-auditor.md`, `cmd_week`] |
| 3 | **Priority honoured** | the named area gets at least 4 more weekly sets than baseline and appears in at least 2 sessions a week, including the first session | add or move an accessory | +1 set on at most 2 items per session, unchecked [code] |
| 4 | **Push and pull balance** | horizontal and vertical pull sets at least equal to push sets, for most people slightly more | add a pull | not checked |
| 5 | **Knee and hip balance** | squat-pattern and hinge-pattern work both present each week, unless a limit excludes one | add the missing pattern | not checked |
| 6 | **The goal's key quality, every week** | strength: a heavy main lift (1 to 6 reps) twice a week · muscle: rule 2 · running: the long run and the easy volume · HYROX: running plus at least one station block · health and older adults: strength twice, balance (60+), aerobic minutes · fat loss: strength twice plus a walking or step target | add the module or flag the plan | partly: modules are attached at `choose`, but the owner saw no HYROX station in session 1 and nothing checks the week |
| 7 | **Second goal present** | the second goal shows up at least once a week | add the module session | not checked |
| 8 | **Excluded regions** | no exercise loads an excluded region; care regions get no impact and nothing advanced | block | in code [code: `build.py` `allowed`] |
| 9 | **Fits the minutes** | within 3 minutes of what was asked | cut in order | in code [code: `fit`] |
| 10 | **Priority survives the cut** | when the clock cuts, the priority accessory is cut last, after the finisher and other accessories | reorder the cut | priority is cut early [code: `fit` order] |
| 11 | **Recovery spacing** | no heavy legs within 24 h of hard cardio; same muscle not trained hard on consecutive days | swap order | first half in code |
| 12 | **Novelty budget** | a beginner meets no more than 2 new exercises a session after session 1, and the main lifts stay all block | hold the variants | main lifts held all block [code]; new-exercise count not checked |
| 13 | **Effort dose by experience** | newcomers no closer than 2 left for the first 2 weeks; nobody at "to the limit" on a heavy barbell lift | soften the effort line | first outing only |
| 14 | **Impact budget** | jumps and runs only when `impact_ok` or not excluded; ramp running by the 10% rule | remove or ramp | in code for running volume |
| 15 | **Plain words** | no method name, abbreviation or slot key reaches the sheet or a message unless they asked | rewrite | law exists, leaks exist (Part 2 row 12) |

## Part 4 · The twelve changes ranked

Ranked by expected effect on **the client's result and the client staying**, the two weighted equally, because a plan quit in week 7 has no result. Costs: **page only** (the web page and `SKILL.md` wording) · **doctrine** (a `doctrine/*.md` rule, program or module json) · **engine** (Python in `engine/`) · **new program** (a new file in `programs/` or `modules/`). None of these repeats a change in `06_interview_audit.md` Part 5.

| rank | change | why it ranks here | cost |
|:-|:-|:-|:-|
| 1 | **Never squeeze silently.** `match` filters out, or clearly marks, any program whose rotation for their days has more sessions than days; when one is still offered, the card and the week line say "4 sessions rotating over your 3 days, each every 9 to 10 days", and the weekly set count uses the real frequency. Better: give `hyrox-prep-4` and `muscle-upperlower-4` true 3-day layouts or drop their `"3"` key. | It is the only place the agent does something wrong, not just less. It cuts weekly volume by about a quarter on the goal muscles and breaks the owner's explicit rule. | engine (small) + doctrine (program layouts) + page (card line) |
| 2 | **Baseline and re-test.** A 2 to 4 item battery chosen by goal and age: push-ups or incline push-ups to form failure, 30 s sit-to-stand, a plank or hang, a 2 km walk or a timed run, plus the main-lift estimates taken from the first outing's logged weights. Recorded as `baseline` in `state.json`, re-run in the last session of every block (4 to 8 weeks), shown side by side. | Feedback against a baseline is the missing half of Michie's effective combination, and "you are 30% stronger than week 1" is the strongest retention line a coach has (Teixeira: competence). Everything in 3 and 8 depends on it. | engine (`plan.py assess`, a test bank) + doctrine (`05_LOADS`, `07_CYCLE`) + page (a test screen) |
| 3 | **The goal contract.** At `choose`: one outcome in their words, one process goal (sessions a week = their days), one performance goal the engine proposes from the baseline (e.g. "squat 1.2 x week 1 by week 8", "5 km without stopping"), and a first milestone at week 3 or 4 that they are very likely to hit. Kept in `PROFILE.md`, read back at every block end. | Multi-component goal setting (McEwan 2016) and goal review (Michie 2009). Gives every later message something to point at. | doctrine (`02`, `07`) + engine (a `goals` field, the proposer) + page |
| 4 | **The weekly check-in with a plan.** When a week closes, one short turn: what went well (the agent names one real win), "which days this week?", "what could get in the way, and what will you do then?". Pushed, not pulled. | Implementation intentions with barrier planning (d about 0.3, larger with barrier planning). The cheapest adherence lever in the literature. | page (the turn) + doctrine (`08_WORKFLOW`) + engine (small: store the days and the if-then) |

## Part 5 · What the agent already does better than many human coaches

Said so the changes above do not break it: the PAR-Q+ gate and the hard-stop rules; progression as tested code per program; readiness that lowers the dose and never lets people pick the session they like; the rotation that never punishes a missed weekday; the clock that counts every rest; the reduced-dose deload with early triggers; independent auditors on every decision. A coach's journey is built on top of these, not instead of them.
